# Mythic Legends — Agent Guidance

## Product context

- Read `docs/GDD.md` before changing gameplay, economy, progression, Arena, Shrine, crafting, inventory, or live-event systems.
- Read `docs/UI_GUIDELINES.md` before changing application menus, category actions, or empty states.
- Do not add excluded systems—eggs, summoning, gacha, Elemental Prism capture, or health-based combat—without explicit approval.
- Keep the MVP's Arena combat non-lethal and positional: knockback, Stamina, hold-to-guard Shields with a minimum-Stamina requirement, temporary knockback immunity, Capture Rings, and the approved elemental sword effects in `docs/GDD.md#elemental-sword-effects`. Preserve the client-reported sword-contact exception in Technical Design; the server owns action/hit eligibility, effects, Stamina, and rewards. New attacks and Shield guard are Arena-only, and both attacker and target must be inside the Arena for a new hit. Do not add ragdoll, health damage, or additional elemental mechanics.
- In the Arena, wild Mythling models do not physically block or support players; player bodies use Roblox's default character collision behavior. Add no custom walking-push forces, contact resistance, or contact Stamina cost. Guard and weapon-knockback immunity do not alter normal body collisions; guard still prevents voluntary walking/jumping. Ordinary contact is not a sword hit or Shield block and does not trigger immunity. Do not make players pass through one another.
- Normal jumps within a Capture Ring keep eligible capture progress, uninterrupted-visit tie priority, and overtime occupancy. Use one server membership rule with horizontal ring bounds and a finite vertical allowance for normal jumps; ground contact is not required. Crossing the ring's edge starts normal decay.
- Deploy with eight players maximum and an initial configurable target of 12 capturable Mythlings in both quiet and full servers. Fill all 12 before capture opens on a fresh server. Replace each captured/despawned contest within three seconds of its removal from availability, including simultaneous removals; this is a maximum refill deadline, not a delay per serial spawn. Count overtime contests but not claimed/escort models, pending attempts, or ended contests. Entering overtime at the normal countdown deadline never triggers replacement. Preserve valid non-overlapping rings and boundary clearance; delayed model cleanup cannot hold capacity, and persistent refill failure must be resolved before release.
- Initial new-spawn chances are 75% Common, 20% Rare, and 5% Epic, with equal chances for all six elements within each rarity. Apply independent selection to fresh-server fill and replacements, preserving the chosen form through placement retries; these are aggregate rarity probabilities, not fixed Arena counts or owned rarity rolls. Configure all six forms within each launch rarity for 20/35/60-second uninterrupted captures respectively. Outside decay immediately removes one second of earned capture progress per second absent, clamped at zero; re-entry preserves remaining progress but starts new tie priority. Keep rates per form and configurable rather than imposing a universal rarity formula.
- Spawn lifetime is separate from capture duration: use a configurable rarity default with an optional named-form override taking precedence. Resolve and fix the server deadline when the spawn becomes capturable; fresh-server timers start when capture opens, never during prefill. Entering/re-entering or later configuration changes do not restart it. For the first trial, set all Common/Rare/Epic lifetime defaults to four minutes (240 seconds), with no named-form overrides across the 18 launch forms. Retain optional custom lifetimes for future content. Validate arrival plus the configured capture against the shared lifetime; default and custom lifetimes both retain empty-ring despawn and occupied-ring overtime. Recheck capture income and evolution pacing with these values; new captures still start at level 1 with 0 XP.
- The MVP has six Mythling chains, one per element, each with three distinct forms: Stage 1/Common → Stage 2/Rare → Stage 3/Epic (18 forms total). This is a launch catalogue mapping, not a universal stage-to-rarity rule. All forms may spawn in the Arena, with higher stages less frequent, and Stage 2/3 may also be obtained through evolution. Evolution is linear and retains the element and owned identity; Stage 3 is final for these launch chains. Legendary and Mythical Mythling content is deferred.
- Mythling rarity is fixed per form, independently of its optional next-evolution target; never roll owned rarity variants or derive rarity from stage. A target may retain or change rarity, and no target means no further evolution while still allowing levels. Standalone Mythlings without evolution chains and shorter, two-form chains are future content; keep the MVP roster at six complete three-form chains, including their final forms with no next evolution. Mythling stages, XP levels, rarity, and Equipment stages remain distinct concepts.
- Mythling and Equipment stage numbers are internal design/configuration shorthand, not player-facing labels, filters, or name suffixes. Present named forms/items, rarity, elements, and relevant statistics; evolution and future Equipment-upgrade previews show the named result and its changes. Keep Mythling XP levels and Shrine levels visible. Launch crafting groups are Swords and Shields.
- Luck, Lucky Yield, and Passive Traits are future-update systems, including the Insomniac and Lucky concepts. New launch captures receive no Luck/Trait rolls, and production has no random bonus or Trait modifiers. Show no launch stats, actions, or placeholders for these systems. Preserve existing saved Luck values and Trait IDs unchanged but inactive; a later design must define their activation and new-acquisition rules. Higher-stage strength comes from configured base Yield, with no extra rarity-based production/XP multiplier or random production-stat bonus.
- Every new Mythling capture retains its caught form and starts at XP level 1 with 0 XP, regardless of stage or rarity. These are new-grant defaults only: evolution, reconnect, reset, and migrations preserve existing earned progression. Launch Stage 3 forms have no further evolution but still level to the normal cap.
- Initial Mythling Yield scaling adds 1% of the current form's base Yield per level above 1, shared across all launch forms and capped at level 100. Use linear growth, not compounding: level 50 is 1.49 times base Yield and level 100 is 1.99 times. Evolution retains the level and applies its bonus to the new form's base Yield; preserve prior earned work and whole-Material output. Keep the coefficient configurable and derive the rate rather than saving a copied stat.
- Initial shared Shrine-work XP is 1 per eligible working second; the next level costs 120 times the current level XP. All forms share this curve, with evolution requirements at levels 6 and 40 and cap 100: 30 minutes, 26 hours, and 165 hours of total eligible work from level 1. Offline work counts; full storage and unassignment pause earnings. Evolution stays manual and preserves level/XP, allowing consecutive eligible evolution actions without retraining. Keep the curve and thresholds configurable.
- Production is deterministic: sum assigned Mythlings' form-and-level Yield and accumulate earned work in fixed accounting batches, identically online and offline. Add new work to retained unfinished progress and grant only whole Materials. Worker changes preserve already earned work and individual activity XP; they cannot recalculate earlier work from the final roster. Keep storage/overflow and exactly-once settlement guarantees. No Luck roll, weighted-Luck accumulator, or offline production bonus is required for launch; migrate legacy pending work without granting a random bonus or erasing earned output/XP.
- Launch Equipment types are one-handed swords and separate Shields only. Additional archetypes, including two-handed weapons, are planned future updates; do not implement their attacks or expose launch placeholders.
- MVP Equipment progression ends at Stage 1 after the plain wooden starter pair. Stage 2 and later upgrades are future updates; do not add launch Equipment-upgrade actions, recipes, or placeholders. Preserve existing player data when deferring prototype content.
- Equipment rarity is fixed per named item and included in the MVP; every copy has the same configured rarity. Resolve it from static item/variant metadata using definition and variant IDs, never a display-name lookup, per-copy roll, or new owned rarity field. Show it alongside the item's name, type, and element; stage remains internal. Preserve existing player data, shared Stage 1 base sword statistics and Shield gameplay values across element variants, and the Stage 2+ deferral.
- Mythlings and Equipment share the ascending rarity order Common → Rare → Epic → Legendary → Mythical, with Mythical highest. Launch wooden starter Equipment is Common; all first-crafted elemental swords and Shields are Rare. Epic/Legendary/Mythical Equipment is future content. These are launch catalogue assignments, not a stage-to-rarity formula. Equipment rarity describes quality, with no extra rarity-based stat, cost, or sell-value multiplier; actual values come from the owning definitions.
- Stage 1 craftable swords and Shields each have six named element variants with fixed matching-Material recipes: Fire Vulcan, Water Triton, Earth Atlas, Air Aura, Light Sol, and Dark Nyx, each followed by Sword or Shield. These names identify variants, not shared tiers. All crafted swords share the wooden sword's base reach, contact geometry, timing, Stamina cost, and horizontal/vertical knockback, with one automatic elemental effect as their advantage. All crafted Shields share gameplay values and have no elemental abilities. Keep the chosen variant on its owned item; crafted and Featured copies behave identically. Future higher-stage progression remains undesigned, and cosmetic Mythling variants remain excluded.
- Apply sword effects only on accepted, unblocked hits; misses, Shield blocks, rejected hits, and immunity-rejected hits grant no effect or Dark refund. Initial configurable values are Fire draining 15 Stamina/second for two seconds; Water reducing walking speed by 25% for two seconds; Earth preventing walking/jumping for 0.75 seconds after landing; Air adding 15% horizontal force to the same hit; Light reducing outgoing horizontal weapon force by 20% for two seconds; and Dark returning three Stamina to the attacker, capped at maximum. Follow `docs/GDD.md#initial-elemental-effect-tuning` for all timing and accounting details. Fire retains normal recovery rules and can force guard down; Dark still requires the full 20-Stamina attack cost upfront. Air/Light do not alter upward launch, Shield slides, or block costs; Light scales total horizontal force after any Air bonus.
- Allow only one active or pending lingering negative effect per target: Fire, Water, Earth, or Light. The first accepted effect wins; later timed effects cannot replace, extend, stack, or queue, while the hit still applies ordinary knockback/immunity. Air and Dark are immediate and can apply against affected targets. Earth's pending root reserves the slot until its first post-hit landing and root completion, or its three-second landing timeout; do not activate on the grounded frame before launch. A completed root grants three seconds of Earth-only protection; an expired pending root grants none. Later hits cannot extend or pause these timers. Water/Earth affect voluntary movement only, never forced knockback or ordinary collisions, and Earth does not prevent attacks or guard.
- Arena crossings and Equipment changes by either player never cleanse, pause, or restart existing or pending elemental effects or their timers. Fire continues outside the Arena, and pending Earth can root on landing there; the landing timeout and Earth recovery window also continue. Reset/disconnect clears the affected character's effects and all of that player's capture progress. Keep active/pending effects readable outside the Arena and Stamina visible during an existing Fire effect there; new combat actions remain unavailable. Describe each sword's effect in Inventory, crafting, and Shop previews.
- Retained future rule: one-handed weapons permit Shields; equipping a two-handed weapon unequips the Shield while preserving its ownership and capacity use. Reject Shield equip and guard with a two-handed weapon equipped, even when sheathed. Switching back permits manual Shield equip without automatic restoration.
- Retained future rule: two-handed weapons trade Shield access for stronger knockback at the same Equipment stage. Stronger force does not automatically break guard or increase the defender's configured block cost. Add slower attacks or higher attack Stamina costs only if testing warrants them; exact tuning, archetype selection, and update order remain open.
- Weapon attacks and Shield guard are mutually exclusive; the server must reject conflicting action requests in both directions. Stamina does not regenerate while the Shield is raised, and time spent guarding cannot be credited as recovery after lowering.
- Initial Shield targets from full Stamina under continuous guard, without an existing Fire burn or other Stamina changes, are three accepted paid blocks for the wooden starter Shield and four for every first-crafted elemental Shield, including Featured copies. The final hit is blocked with the normal reduced slide and immunity, then its full cost forces protection off and lowering because remaining Stamina is below the guard threshold. Rejected hits spend no block Stamina. Achieve these counts through configured maximum Stamina, impact costs, and guard thresholds, not a separate durability or block-charge system. Initial maximum and character-spawn Stamina are 100; the wooden Shield's block cost and guard minimum are 30 each, and the crafted Shield's are 25 each. Partial Stamina and ring displacement can reduce how long a player holds position, and raising/equipping does not grant a fresh block allowance.
- Initial wooden and first-crafted swords cost 20 Stamina per accepted swing, hit or miss, with a one-second minimum interval between accepted starts. Recovery is 10 Stamina per second while the Shield is fully lowered, including during sword attacks; raising, guarding, and lowering recover nothing. From zero, two fully lowered seconds afford one attack and ten fill the bar without spending. With one-second starts, no guarding, and no elemental Stamina changes, recovery allows nine swings at seconds 0–8, then another at second 10; respect unfinished animation/action locks rather than forcing that cadence. Arena entry/re-entry and Equipment changes never refill Stamina or reset action limits; initialize the configured value on actual character spawn. Guard transitions, contact/animation timing, immunity, and baseline knockback remain open for tuning alongside the approved elemental effects.
- Every Base includes one permanent, ready-to-use Crafting Station outside its two starting build slots. Build slots are only for Shrines; each expansion costs Gold plus fixed equal quantities of all six normal Materials and permanently adds one slot, up to six unlocked slots in the MVP. Dismantling, reset, and reconnect retain purchased slots. Inventory-capacity upgrades also require Gold plus fixed equal quantities of all six normal Materials, with no substitutions. Shrine upgrades use Gold plus matching normal Material. Preserve existing purchased upgrades through cost changes without retroactive charges.
- Initial Inventory slot limits are Materials 12 → 24 → 36, Mythlings 24 → 36 → 48, and Equipment 12 → 24 → 36. Each category has two sequential, independently purchased +12-slot upgrades; each category's first upgrade costs 20,000 Gold plus 50 of each of the six normal Materials, and its final upgrade costs 300,000 Gold plus 200 of each. All six normal Materials stack to 1,000 per type per slot; round occupied slots up separately for each Material ID. Assigned Mythlings, equipped Equipment, and the protected starter pair still count against their category limits. Preserve crafting output/refund reservations and require every upgrade's ingredients to fit before granting its capacity. Resolve capacities from configuration and purchased upgrade state; do not save copied static limits. Show maximum capacity after the final upgrade, without implying Shop refresh unlocks more.
- Paid upgrades are multi-day saving goals. Shrine level 1 to 2 initially costs 1,000 Gold plus 400 matching normal Materials for all six elements, roughly 2–3 full daily collections from one or two productive level-1 Shrines without prior savings or extra income. Shrine level 2 to 3 costs 15,000 Gold plus 4,000 matching Materials: about five full daily collections from two productive level-2 Shrines or ten from one under the same saving assumptions. Accumulate payments in Inventory across collections; keep Shrine storage unchanged. Starting Inventory must hold the first payment without upgrading; reachable pre-purchase capacity must hold the second. Base expansions to slots 3/4/5/6 initially cost 10,000/50,000/150,000/500,000 Gold plus 50/100/150/200 of each of the six normal Materials respectively. Gold sets most Base/Inventory saving time, while Shrine upgrades remain the large Material-saving purchases. Missing-element Shop inputs require 5/10/15/20 allowances for Base expansions and 5/20 for Inventory upgrades; all missing types may be bought during the same visits. Preserve duplicate-element Base layouts without requiring dismantling or matching Shrine ownership for these purchases. Validate pre-upgrade production, reachable elements, Shop stock, and Inventory capacity; retained Materials cannot also count as Gold-sale income. Do not add waiting timers or change the initial build, craft, or free evolution rules.
- All six elemental Shrine types share one MVP upgrade path: levels 1/2/3 provide 1/2/3 Mythling slots and initial shared storage of 300/1,200/3,600 whole Materials, purchased with Gold plus matching normal Material. Capacity depends on Shrine level, not the assigned team; the level-3 daily collection reference is three developed Epics, not a guaranteed timer. No separate slot/storage upgrade tracks or Shrine production multiplier. Preserve assignments, stored output, unfinished production, and earned XP when upgrading; new slots start empty.
- Initial base Yield is 12/18/32 normal Materials/hour for each launch Common/Rare/Epic form respectively, across all six elements. Store these configurable trial values per named form, without a runtime rarity multiplier. Nominal production is one Material per 300/200/112.5 eligible working seconds before level scaling. The Epic trial rate preserves approximately daily collection after removing Luck/Traits: three Epics starting at level 50 with 0 XP toward the next level fill 3,600 storage in roughly 24.2 working hours, including later level gains. Five recipe Materials take 25 minutes at the unchanged Common base rate. Keep fixed production batches and activity-based XP independent of item completion; these times are not new batch intervals. Online/offline rates are identical. All launch sword/Shield recipes initially take 60 seconds after starting, separate from ingredient production. Preserve active jobs' recorded deadlines and follow server-derived UI estimates.
- All six normal launch Materials can be sold from Inventory in selected whole quantities at low fixed configured unit Gold prices. Collect Shrine output first, including offline output; do not auto-convert production to Gold. Keep duplicate Mythling captures the main early Gold source. Material Sell is the amber primary action; Discard remains secondary and grants no Gold. Sales atomically remove owned quantity and grant Gold, never consume Shrine storage or crafting refund reservations, and never release a job's reservations.
- Mythling sale value is fixed in each named form's metadata. Initial launch payouts are 25 Gold for each Common form, 100 for each Rare form, and 300 for each Epic form. XP level, inactive legacy Luck/Trait data, and acquisition route never modify the payout. Evolution uses the new current form's configured value, with no runtime rarity/stage multiplier or owned sale-price field. Validate ownership and unassignment, reject stale form/price selections, and atomically remove the selected instance and grant Gold once. Final-copy sales remain allowed.
- The Gold Shop has Materials, Featured, and Inventory Upgrades. Always list all six normal Materials with limited personal stock that replenishes on one shared schedule; rotate Featured offers of existing first-crafted elemental swords/Shields at a premium over buying their recipe Materials plus crafting Gold. Keep all six variants, including Earth's Atlas pair. Preserve stock usage across reconnect/reset/server changes, protect crafting reservations, and atomically charge Gold, grant the exact item/quantity, and consume stock. Refresh must not reset Inventory upgrades or silently substitute a stale offer. Purchases grant no Shrine-work XP or automatic equip. Validate buy/sell/craft prices together to prevent resale profit loops; higher Equipment content remains deferred.
- Initial configurable Shop tuning is an hourly shared refresh for Materials and Featured, 10 of each Material at every Base size for one matching sword-and-Shield recipe pair, and one Featured sword plus one Featured Shield of the same element with one copy each per player. Rotate the matching pair Fire → Water → Earth → Air → Light → Dark → repeat, one element per hour and six hours per full cycle. All servers share the same hourly period and Featured element; joining/reset/server changes never restart the cycle or personal allowances, and missed or unused stock does not accumulate. Do not scale Material stock with unlocked Base slots. Follow the GDD's initial economy values for all six elements: 100 starting Gold, a 100-Gold basic Shrine, and a 50-Gold/five-Material recipe producing one sword or Shield. Normal Materials buy/sell for 10/2 Gold; Featured Equipment costs 150 and the same named Equipment sells for 25 whether crafted or bought. These meet the 50% Featured premium and 20% Material resale targets. Preserve starter sale protection and existing balances; tune timings before claiming the initial prices meet progression goals. Waiting after using an allowance is intentional return-visit pacing; retain the independent first-craft route from every starting Shrine.
- Combat Consumables, the six-slot Hotbar, and expanded crafting queues/station upgrades are planned future updates, not MVP requirements. Launch crafting allows one active Equipment job per Base. Preserve existing player data when deferring prototype features.

## Architecture

- Read `docs/CONVENTIONS.md` before changing source code or project structure. It owns the repository layout, Rojo hierarchy, naming, typing, cleanup, formatting, and logging conventions.
- Read `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` before changing architecture, networking, combat implementation, persistence, configuration schemas, or UI code ownership. It owns implementation contracts; `README.md` owns setup and verification commands.
- Keep capture, currency, production, crafting, inventory, and permission checks server-authoritative. Divine Intervention is post-launch and must not be implemented or expanded without explicit approval.
- Store static content and balance data in `src/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Configurations`.
- Persist only mutable player state and metadata IDs. Do not duplicate static metadata in player saves.
- Keep earned and player-created mutable values in saved player state. Preserve legacy individually rolled Mythling Luck and Trait IDs without requiring or activating those deferred fields for new launch captures.
- Keep tuning values configurable; do not hard-code economy, production, cooldown, spawn, or event-balance values into client logic.
